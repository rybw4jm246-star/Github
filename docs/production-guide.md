# Production Guide

This is the standing style guide for every episode. It exists so a large catalog of
videos, made faceless and mostly unsupervised, still sounds and looks like one
channel and never overclaims. Read it before writing a script, and re-check a
finished script against it before marking an episode done.

## What this channel is and isn't

- **Is:** history, philosophy, linguistics, and lived practice, explained from the
  Vedic texts, for people who know nothing about them.
- **Isn't:** religious preaching, mythology presented as literal fact, politics,
  sectarian debate, or claims that the Vedas "predicted" modern science.
- When a topic invites a sensational claim (e.g. "the Vedas predicted the Big
  Bang"), the episode's job is to explain what the text actually says, why the
  claim is tempting, and where it overreaches — not to repeat the claim uncritically.

## Audience

Ages 16–55, curious about history, philosophy, ancient civilizations, meditation,
psychology, productivity, yoga, science, or spirituality — but starting from zero
knowledge of the Vedas.

## Length and tone

3–5 minutes. Documentary tone: Kurzgesagt's clarity, Johnny Harris's and
MagnatesMedia's pacing and visual density, Dhruv Rathee's plain-language framing —
applied to Vedic material specifically.

## Required script sections (all 13, every episode)

1. Curiosity hook (first 5 seconds)
2. Problem statement
3. Historical background
4. Original Sanskrit verse (Devanagari)
5. Transliteration (IAST or simplified Roman)
6. Hindi translation
7. English translation
8. Historical / scholarly interpretation
9. Modern application
10. Practical takeaway
11. One surprising fact
12. Strong ending
13. Call to subscribe

Every episode ends with the fixed line: **"What ancient wisdom should we explore
next?"**

## Accuracy rules

- Every factual claim carries its source: **Veda, Mandala, Sukta, Mantra** (or the
  equivalent locator for non-Rigvedic texts — Kanda/Adhyaya for Yajurveda in its
  prose portions, Kanda/Sukta for Atharvaveda, Kanda/Adhyaya for Samaveda archika
  structure).
- Never fabricate a citation. If a verse can't be pinned to a specific location,
  cut the claim.
- Where scholars disagree on meaning or dating, say so explicitly: *"Scholars
  interpret this differently."* Name the range of views in one sentence; don't
  pretend to resolve it.
- Translations should track known scholarly renderings (e.g. Griffith, Jamison &
  Brereton, Wendy Doniger) in substance; when the script's English wording is the
  writer's own paraphrase rather than a direct quotation, say so.
- Distinguish clearly between what the text says and what later tradition or
  popular culture has claimed about it.

## Per-sentence visual spec

For every sentence of narration, the script's visual column specifies:

- AI image prompt
- Camera movement
- Animation
- Color palette
- Lighting
- B-roll
- Motion graphics
- Sound design
- Background music

## Narration

Hindi and English narration are written out in full, separately (not as
translations of each other done live) — each should read naturally in its own
language.

## Cinematic style

Ancient India, golden sunrise, fire rituals, mountains, rivers, cosmos/stars,
temples, cosmic animation, macro nature, epic transitions, 4K documentary quality.
