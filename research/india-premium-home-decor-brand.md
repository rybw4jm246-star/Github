# KORA: A Premium Indian Home & Lifestyle Brand Plan

*Strategy memo, September 2026. Capital ₹10–15 L · launch in 90 days · Shopify, Amazon, Instagram, pop-ups, interior designers and boutiques first; Myntra and Ajio Luxe later.*

> **How to read this.** Sourcing prices, MOQs and lead times are **typical artisan and exporter ranges for these clusters, as of mid-2026, based on category knowledge**. They are not quotes, and I did not scrape Pinterest, Etsy or retailer data live for this memo. Prices swing ±30% with material (brass price per kg, marble grade), finish and order size. Treat every number as a starting point for negotiation and confirm it with samples before you place a PO. **The GST treatment of handicrafts changed with GST 2.0 (22 Sep 2025).** This memo assumes **5%** for most handicraft, textile, wood, grass, paper and pottery items and **18%** for brass/metal articles, glass, lighting, clocks, furniture and fragrance. Confirm each HSN code with your CA; several brass items may qualify for 5% as handicrafts, which would be upside. "KORA" and the other names are suggestions and have **not** been trademark-searched.

## 1. The opportunity in one paragraph

Indian premium home décor has two poles and an empty middle.
- **Maximal heritage luxury**, typified by Good Earth: ₹5,000–₹50,000, ornate, occasion-driven.
- **Mass décor**: Home Centre, IKEA, Amazon private labels and Moradabad exporters' Amazon storefronts, at ₹300–₹1,500, generic and trend-chasing.

Modern Indian lifestyle brands such as Nicobar, Ellementry, The Wishing Chair and Nestasia have proven that urban 25–45-year-olds will pay ₹1,500–₹5,000 for design-led objects. **No one owns the "Quiet Indian" aesthetic:** tonal, material-first and un-ornamented, one craft per object, Japandi arrived at *through* India rather than borrowed from Scandinavia. That is the Ferm Living / Zara Home position, built on Indian materials that those brands actually buy from Moradabad, Jaipur and Panipat anyway.

**The positioning:**
> **KORA — Objects for a slower Indian home.**
> *Kora* (कोरा) means raw, unbleached, untouched: the undyed state of handwoven cotton. Every KORA object is the craft reduced to its quietest, truest form.

**Design rules** (what makes it "not a handicraft store"):
1. **Material over motif.** Show the brass, stone, clay or fibre. Drop the ornament, or reduce it to a single tonal gesture.
2. **Earth palette only:** kora (undyed), mitti (clay), kaajal (charcoal), haldi (muted ochre), neem (sage), and brass as the only metal.
3. **Architecture as the design language.** Indian arches, stepwells, jaalis and domes, abstracted into objects: the "Indian architectural object" is the signature.
4. **One object, one craft, one artisan cluster, one story.** Every product has a place of origin on the label.
5. **Rituals, not rooms.** Collections are built around daily rituals (morning chai, lighting a lamp, reading, bathing, the first monsoon rain), which is how our customer actually lives and posts.

**Alternative names** (check trademarks in classes 3, 4, 8, 16, 20, 21, 24 and 35): *Aangan & Co.*, *Chhaya Studio*, *Maati House*, *Nīla*.

## 3. Customer and research signals

**Customer:** 25–45, ₹12–50 LPA, living in metros, tier-1 cities and premium tier-2 cities (Chandigarh, Indore, Kochi, Coimbatore, Jaipur, Lucknow). Rents or owns a 2–3 BHK. Pins "Japandi living room", "earthy home", "slow living". Gifts 15–25 times a year: weddings, housewarmings, Diwali, corporate. Spends ₹30k–₹1.5L a year on home and gifting.

**What the reference brands and channels tell us** (qualitative, from category knowledge):

| Source | Signal | What it means for KORA |
|---|---|---|
| Pinterest / Instagram interiors | "Japandi", "wabi-sabi", "earthy minimal", "arch décor", "travertine", "fluted", "ribbed glass", "brass accents" are evergreen aesthetic clusters, not one-season fads | Fluted marble, arch objects, ribbed Firozabad glass and tonal brass are the Indian versions of the most-pinned materials |
| Anthropologie / Zara Home / West Elm / CB2 | Much of their brass, block print, jute and marble is *sourced from India* (Moradabad, Jaipur, Panipat, Bhadohi) | The supply chain is proven. The gap is **brand ownership of origin** |
| Ferm Living / Menu / Hay | Small architectural objects (bookends, candle holders, trays) sell as "collectible sculpture" at high margin | The KORA hero category is the **Indian architectural object** |
| Etsy top sellers | Handmade-paper journals, wax-seal kits, brass incense holders, block-printed napkins and cabinet knobs sell strongly internationally | Low-breakage, high-margin export SKUs |
| Boutique hotels / cafés / Airbnbs (Jaipur, Goa, Auroville, Himachal) | Lime plaster, terracotta, kora cotton, brass, cane: the "Indian slow-stay" look | Build a B2B hospitality channel and an "as seen in" credibility layer |
| Wedding & corporate gifting | Shifting from dry-fruit boxes and silver to **curated, sustainable, experiential** gift boxes | Gift boxes are a core product, not an afterthought |
| Museum shops (global) | Buy architecture-inspired, story-rich objects | Export route for the stepwell and arch pieces |

**Hidden products** (rarely sold in a modern, branded form):
- **Mitti-attar room mist** (Kannauj's distilled "smell of first rain"): a scent story that belongs only to India.
- **Longpi black-stone teaware** (Manipur): wabi-sabi before wabi-sabi, and barely retailed outside the Northeast.
- **Gamcha, reimagined** as a premium quick-dry bath and beach towel: the "Turkish towel" of India.
- **Tonal blue-pottery cabinet knobs** (Anthropologie's knob category, from Jaipur, in modern glazes).
- **Stepwell (Chand Baori) brass candle holder**: a signature architectural object.
- **Thathera hammered-brass tumblers**: Jandiala Guru's craft is on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list.
- **Bidri card holders and Kalpi letter-writing kits**: premium corporate gifting.
- **Sarthebari bell-metal meditation bowl**: the Indian answer to the Tibetan singing bowl.
- **Kauna-reed and Moonj-grass lidded baskets**: natural-fibre storage with a strong origin story.
- **Aranmula metal mirror**: a GI-tagged, signed collector edition.

### Foundation product notes

**1. Mitti room mist and diffuser**
- **Story:** Kannauj has distilled *mitti attar* for centuries by baking monsoon clay and capturing its vapour in sandalwood oil, the "smell of first rain". It is the most Indian scent that exists, and no premium home brand owns it.
- **Build:** Buy attar from a traditional distiller. Blend into an alcohol- or water-based room spray in 100 ml amber glass with a letterpress label.
- **Compliance:** Room sprays fall outside the cosmetic rules, but check the Legal Metrology labelling, and the excise/state rules if you use alcohol.
- **Custom / private label:** Full.
- **Packaging:** Molded-pulp sleeve.
- **Shipping:** Medium difficulty (liquid, glass). Air couriers may restrict alcohol-based sprays, so prefer a water-based formula.
- **Extensions:** Monsoon candle, linen water, car diffuser, hotel amenities (B2B).
- **Moat:** A signature scent becomes the brand's smell. **Put a scent strip in every box.**

**2. Incense**
- **Sourcing:** Mysuru/Bengaluru agarbatti makers do private label at 500-box MOQs.
- **Formula:** Charcoal-free, masala-rolled, natural resins. Scents: *Petrichor*, *Vetiver & Smoke*, *Temple Champa*, *Monsoon Chai*.
- **Repeat:** Monthly subscription "Sacred Spaces Refill" at ₹449/month.
- **Risks:** Minimal breakage, flat-pack shipping, lowest price point (the customer-acquisition entry product).

**3. Candles**
- **Build:** Khurja makes the reactive-glaze vessel; a Bengaluru or Puducherry contract chandler pours it. Or pour in-house: equipment costs about ₹60k and teaches you the craft.
- **Refill model:** Customers mail back the vessel, or buy a refill pour. This makes the vessel a collectible that sits on the shelf.
- **Breakage:** Medium (ceramic); needs a molded-pulp insert.

**4. Stepwell candle holder (the brand icon)**
- **Design:** A stepped, inverted-pyramid brass form abstracted from Chand Baori (Abhaneri, Rajasthan). It holds a tealight or taper.
- **Why it matters:** It photographs like a Ferm Living object and tells an Indian story in one glance.
- **Protection:** Register the design under the Designs Act, 2000 (about ₹1,000–₹4,000 official fee per design for individuals/startups; confirm current fees).
- **Sourcing:** Moradabad casting or sheet fabrication; custom patterns take 2–3 sampling rounds. Finish: brushed raw brass with lacquer.
- **Collection line:** Jaali, Jharokha, Gumbad (dome) and Chhatri pieces become the **"Architecture of India" collector series**, with one new form per season, numbered.

**5–7. Marble and sandstone architectural objects**
- **Clusters:** Makrana (white marble), Kishangarh (the marble trading hub, fluted trays), Dholpur (sandstone).
- **Shipping:** Heavy (1–3 kg), so the shipping cost is about ₹120–₹200 per order. Price it in.
- **Breakage:** Medium. Chips happen on corners, so specify rounded edges and use molded pulp.
- **Custom:** CNC shops will cut your exact CAD at MOQ 50.
- **Export:** Strong. Global museum shops and design stores stock arch objects.

**8. Longpi**
- **The craft:** Black serpentine stone plus weathered rock, hand-shaped without a wheel, from Ukhrul district, Manipur.
- **Supply:** Supply is small and artisan-led, so work with a cooperative and pay advances.
- **Limits:** Breakage medium, lead time long, low customization. Sell as a **limited "Himalayan & Northeast" drop**, not always-in-stock.
- **Role:** Prestige more than volume.

**9. Kansa**
- **Story:** Bell metal (copper + tin) from Kantilo (Odisha) or Sarthebari (Assam). Ayurvedic tradition, wedding-trousseau heritage.
- **Margins:** Metal prices make GM lower (~60%), but AOV is high and gifting demand is huge.
- **Pricing:** Price wedding sets at ₹6,990–₹12,990.

**10. Firozabad glass**
- **Supply:** India's glass city. Mouth-blown ribbed and fluted forms in clear, amber, smoke and sage.
- **Breakage:** High, so ship only direct-to-consumer at first (not Amazon FBA) with custom molded pulp. Run drop tests before launch.

**11. Gamcha**
- **Story:** The humble red-and-white checked cotton towel of Bengal and Assam, redesigned in tonal checks (kora/charcoal, kora/indigo) at larger sizes as a quick-dry bath, beach and gym towel.
- **Positioning:** *"The Turkish towel is Indian."*
- **Why it wins:** Easy to ship, repeat purchase, strong export story.

**12–14. Textiles**
- **Clusters:** Bagru dabu (mud-resist), Ajrakhpur Ajrakh (natural indigo and madder), Bhujodi Kala cotton (indigenous rain-fed organic cotton), Varanasi tonal brocade.
- **Private label:** Full; weavers and printers accept your colourways from 30–100 metres.
- **Shipping:** Zero breakage, lightweight. Textiles are the **highest-volume export category** and the easiest wholesale conversation with boutiques.

**15. Hardware (knobs and pulls)**
- **Why:** Interior designers specify knobs by the hundred. A designer trade program turns one kitchen project into 40–80 units.
- **Design:** Blue pottery in *non-traditional* tonal glazes (bone, sage, rust) avoids the "tourist blue" look.

**16. Baskets**
- **Clusters:** Moonj grass (Prayagraj, GI-tagged), golden grass (Kendrapara), kauna reed (Manipur).
- **Shipping:** Light; nest the sets to cut volumetric weight.

**17–18. Paper**
- **Clusters:** Sanganer and Kalpi are India's handmade-paper capitals (cotton rag).
- **Products:** Journals, letter kits with a brass wax seal, and a 12-month artisan calendar.
- **Why:** Corporate gifting orders of 100–1,000 units with logo debossing. The calendar is an annual repeat purchase.

**19. Incense accessories**
- **Role:** Attach-rate products, bought together with incense (#2).

**20. Gift boxes**
- **Why:** The single biggest revenue lever in Indian premium décor (weddings, Diwali, corporate).
- **Sizes:** Pre-built boxes at three price points (₹2,990 / ₹4,990 / ₹7,990) plus "build your own".

## 7. Moat: why competitors can't simply copy KORA

A Moradabad exporter can copy a brass candle holder in three weeks. Physical products are not the moat. The system around them is:

1. **Exclusive artisan partnerships.**
   - Sign 2–3 year exclusivity on *your designs* (not the craft) with 8–12 anchor workshops.
   - Pay 30–50% advances and guarantee minimum annual volumes. Artisans stay loyal to buyers who pay on time; this is the cheapest, strongest moat in Indian craft.
2. **Registered designs + trademarks.**
   - Register every hero form (stepwell, arch, dome) under the Designs Act.
   - File trademarks in classes 3, 4, 8, 16, 20, 21, 24 and 35.
   - Enrol in Amazon Brand Registry (a TM application number is enough to enrol).
3. **Signature sensory identity.** The mitti scent in every box, a consistent tactile packaging, and the brand palette. Copycats can clone an object, not a feeling.
4. **QR provenance and craft certificate.** Every piece carries a card with the artisan's name, cluster, map pin, process video and a numbered certificate. Where the craft is GI-tagged (Moonj, Longpi, Kutch embroidery, Bidri, Aranmula), register as an authorised user.
5. **The collector series "Architecture of India".** One numbered architectural object per season in limited runs (500 pieces), signed by the artisan. It creates scarcity and a reason to come back.
6. **Designer collaborations.** One Indian architect or designer collaboration per year (a capsule of 3–5 objects).
7. **Refill ecosystems.** Candles, incense, mist and diffuser refills lock in repeat purchase that decorative objects alone never create.
8. **Trade program.** 20–25% trade discount and specification sheets for interior designers and architects. Once your knobs are in a designer's spec library, you are sticky.
9. **Speed and seasonality.** Four collection drops a year plus two limited editions. The copycat is always one season behind.

## 8. Packaging and unboxing

**Principles:**
- Plastic-free.
- Reusable (the box and dust bag get a second life).
- **The inside is prettier than the outside.** The outside ships; the inside is photographed.

**Unboxing sequence:**
1. **Outer shipper:** plain kraft, 5-ply corrugated, one-colour stamp logo. Printed inside the lid: *"Slow down. This was made by hand, somewhere in India."*
2. **Scent:** a mitti-attar scent strip under the lid, so the first thing they meet is the smell of rain.
3. **Wrap:** Sanganer handmade-paper wrap with a debossed seal sticker (upgrade to a wax seal for gift orders).
4. **Dust bag:** the object sits in a **kora cotton drawstring dust bag** with the logo in tonal thread. The bag is reused, and it is itself Instagrammable.
5. **Provenance card:** letterpress on cotton-rag paper, with the artisan's name, cluster map, craft story and QR.
6. **Care card**, plus a handwritten-style gift note if gifted.
7. **Gift upgrade (+₹199):** a rigid box wrapped in handmade paper, a jute-thread tie, a dried leaf or flower, and a wax seal.

**Protective spec by breakage class:**

| Class | Products | Protection | Channel rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| L (textiles, brass, grass, paper) | ~55% of range | Dust bag + rigid kraft box, no inner cushioning needed | All channels incl. Amazon FBA |
| M (marble, stone, ceramic, Longpi) | ~30% | **Molded-pulp (bagasse) custom insert** + double box, 5 cm void clearance; drop-test from 1 m on 6 faces and 3 corners before launch | D2C + Amazon Easy Ship; FBA only after passing tests |
| H (glass, terracotta, mirrors) | ~15% | Custom pulp cradle + honeycomb paper, double box | D2C (own courier) only for year 1; build in a 3% breakage allowance |

| Element | Direction |

**Photography style:**
- **Light:** natural window light only, long raking shadows, early morning or late afternoon.
- **Surfaces:** lime plaster, Kota stone, raw linen, unpolished wood, terracotta floors.
- **Composition:** one object, one frame, generous negative space; 4:5 for Instagram, 2:3 for Pinterest.
- **Human presence:** hands, never faces for product shots. Artisans' hands at work are the second visual pillar.
- **Locations:** a Jaipur haveli courtyard, a Goan Portuguese-era home, Auroville-style rammed-earth rooms, a Mumbai apartment with a balcony (the "real home" look).
- **Grading:** warm, muted, slightly desaturated; subtle film grain; no hard flash, no pure white seamless backgrounds except on Amazon main images.
- **Avoid:** festive clutter, marigolds everywhere, brass idols, "ethnic" stereotypes. India is shown through material and light, not cliché.

### 10.4 Branding strategy
- **Brand idea:** *The Quiet Indian Home.* The world knows loud India; KORA shows the quiet one.
- **Voice:** Calm, precise, poetic, but never "ethnic" or preachy. Short sentences. Hindi words used sparingly as design accents (*kora, mitti, aangan, dhoop*).
- **Architecture:** Masterbrand **KORA** → collections (named by ritual or landscape) → objects (named after places: "Abhaneri candle holder", "Makrana arch", "Phulia towel").
- **Proof points on every page:** place of origin, craft, artisan, time to make, material, care.
- **Social impact without charity-speak:** publish an "Artisan Ledger" once a year (artisans partnered with, share of revenue paid to them, advances paid). Don't claim percentages you can't audit.

### 10.7 Pricing strategy
- **Multiplier:** 4.5–6× landed cost on D2C (the higher multiple on small objects and consumables, the lower on high-material-cost pieces like kansa and throws).
- **Price endings:** ₹…90 (₹990, ₹1,490, ₹2,990) instead of ₹…99, which reads as more premium.
- **Channel parity:** same MRP on Shopify and Amazon. D2C wins on exclusives, gift wrap, bundles and the collector series, not on discounts.
- **Discount discipline:** no sitewide sales. Allowed levers: bundles (3 for 2 on incense/refills), the gift-box premium, a first-order welcome at 10% max, and an end-of-season "Studio Seconds" for cosmetic flaws (honest, and on-brand).
- **Trade:** 20–25% off MRP for registered interior designers and architects.
- **Wholesale:** boutiques at 50% of MRP (requires ≥4.5× cost multiplier, which is why sub-4× SKUs stay D2C-only).
- **Export:** FOB at 2–2.5× landed cost.
- **Corporate:** tiered by volume (100+, 500+, 1,000+ units), with branding (deboss, sleeve, card) charged separately.

### 10.9 Inventory plan
- **ABC classification from Month 2:**
  - **A:** top ~20% of SKUs, ~60% of revenue. Hold 8 weeks of stock and re-order at 5 weeks' cover.
  - **B:** 6 weeks of stock.
  - **C:** 4 weeks of stock, or made-to-order.
- **Lead-time rule:** re-order point = (average weekly sales × lead time in weeks) + 2 weeks' safety stock. Artisan lead times are 20–45 days, and they **stretch 2–3 weeks before Diwali and during harvest and wedding months**, when labour goes home.
- **Seasonality:** terracotta and Longpi drying slows in the monsoon, so order Monsoon-collection clay goods by April.
- **Made-to-order / pre-order:** collector series, kansa wedding sets and large textiles at 50% advance. This cuts dead stock and funds production.
- **Dead-stock policy:** after 120 days, bundle into gift boxes → Studio Seconds → corporate sets. Never discount heroes.
- **Tools:** Shopify inventory + a simple Google Sheet PO tracker (Months 1–6); move to an inventory tool like Zoho Inventory or Unicommerce when you go multi-channel (Month 6+).
- **Fulfilment:** self-ship from a 300–500 sq ft unit for Months 1–6 (quality control over the unboxing); a 3PL with kitting from Month 6–9, or when orders pass ~40/day.

### 10.11 Risks and mitigation

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Quality inconsistency** (handmade variance, lacquer peeling, dimension drift) | High | Written specs + a golden sample at each workshop; AQL 2.5 pre-dispatch QC; tell customers that variation is a feature, but control the tolerances |
| **Breakage in transit** | Medium–High | Breakage classes; molded pulp; drop tests; keep Class H off FBA; 3% breakage reserve in pricing; courier insurance for orders over ₹5k |
| **Design copying by exporters / Amazon sellers** | High | Design registrations; speed (4 drops a year); story, packaging and scent the copier can't reproduce; Brand Registry takedowns |
| **Artisan capacity or delays** (festivals, harvest, weather) | High | Two workshops per hero SKU; advances; pre-season ordering; realistic launch dates |
| **Cash-flow crunch** from MOQs | Medium | Small first runs; pre-orders; paying the balance on QC; consumables with fast turns; a reserve of ≥ ₹1.5 L |
| **High CAC on Meta** | Medium–High | Organic-first (reels + Pinterest); creator seeding; gifting and corporate B2B for lower CAC; entry-priced consumables for acquisition |
| **COD returns / RTO** | Medium | Prepaid incentive (free gift wrap); COD only above a threshold with confirmation calls; limit COD on fragile items |
| **Brass price volatility** | Medium | Quarterly price reviews; design lighter-gauge forms; mix brass with marble or wood |
| **Compliance** (Legal Metrology labels, GST HSN, alcohol-based sprays, candle/diffuser safety labelling, cosmetics rules if you sell soap) | Medium | CA + compliance checklist before launch; avoid soap and skin products in year 1; water-based room mist |
| **"Handicraft store" drift** (range sprawl) | Medium | Every SKU must pass the 5 design rules in §1; kill 20% of SKUs each season |
| **Ethical / fair-pay credibility** | Low–Medium | Pay on time; advances; publish the Artisan Ledger; never claim certifications you don't hold |

## 11. The path to ₹100 Cr (realistic view)

₹100 Cr is a **5–7 year outcome**, not a year-1 target. A disciplined path:

| Stage | Years | Revenue engine | Indicative revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prove | 1 | D2C + Instagram + gifting | **₹40–80 L** (base case; ₹1 Cr+ with strong corporate gifting) |
| Grow | 2–3 | Amazon + corporate + trade + Myntra/Ajio Luxe + international D2C/Etsy | ₹5–15 Cr |
| Scale | 4–5 | 3–5 flagship stores / shop-in-shops, export wholesale (global museum shops and concept stores), hospitality B2B | ₹30–60 Cr |
| Brand | 5–7 | Home-fragrance line becomes ~25–30% of revenue (repeat); furniture-lite and lighting; collaborations | ₹100 Cr+ |

**The two things that decide it:**
1. **A repeat engine.** Fragrance, candles and refills, paper and linens must reach at least 30% of revenue; decorative objects alone rarely repeat.
2. **Gifting as a system.** Corporate, wedding and festival gifting are India's largest premium home-goods occasions, and the only way to get large orders early.
